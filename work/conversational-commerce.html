02 / Independent product engineering

Conversational commerce

A shopping system that carries requirements across turns, retrieves eligible products, and explains recommendations using catalog evidence.

Role
Creator & engineer
Scope
Catalog ETL · backend · product integration
Access
Private tester demo

The problem

Shopping requirements evolve with the conversation.

A shopper might start with a broad need, add a budget, and then compare two results. I built a commerce backend and catalog pipeline that keep the shopping task across turns and ground explanations in the supplied product listings.

Explore the steps. This example makes no live AI requests.

  1. 01Audit & normalizeDuckDB processes historical fashion and beauty metadata, preserving source evidence and unknown fields.
  2. 02Publish a catalogChecksummed Parquet and JSONL outputs feed an immutable, validated PostgreSQL snapshot.
  3. 03Build retrievalReusable embedding snapshots and verified Qdrant collections support dense and BM25 retrieval.
  4. 04Interpret & recommendPersistent shopping requirements, mandatory filters, rank fusion, and configurable reranking.
  5. 05Explain & continueListing-grounded highlights, comparisons, streaming events, and saved conversation history.

My contribution

The conversation is only one part of the system.

Reproducible data preparation

A separate ETL repository handles normalization, coherent duplicate selection, quality-eligible subsets, provenance, checksums, and atomic publication. It prepares the catalog; the backend owns models and embeddings.

Retrieval with explicit boundaries

PostgreSQL is authoritative for product records and mandatory filters. Qdrant serves dense and BM25 candidates, with reciprocal-rank fusion and configurable local or hosted reranking.

Conversation lifecycle

SQLite transcripts, recent turns, rolling summaries, persistent shopping tasks, request replay protection, and streamed product cards and highlights.

Private product experience

The shared Querix frontend supports tester sign-in, assigned categories, saved chats, and administrative account management. The commerce demo requires access.

Grounding & data boundaries

Keep unknowns visible.

The catalog uses historical product metadata. Its observed prices and listing claims do not establish current prices, live stock, purchasable variants, or independently verified suitability. Explanations should stay within the evidence supplied by the catalog.

Two products, distinct retrieval stacks.

Querix Search uses pgvector and a persistent lexical index. Conversational Commerce uses Qdrant for online dense and BM25 retrieval, backed by an authoritative PostgreSQL catalog. They share a product website while keeping their service and access contracts separate.
